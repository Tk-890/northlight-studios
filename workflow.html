What is a workflow?

A workflow is simply the series of steps a task follows from beginning to completion. It explains what happens, who or what does each step, and what needs to happen next.

THE SIMPLE VERSION

A repeatable path from start to finish.

Think about what happens when a new customer contacts your business. Someone receives the message, reads it, records the details, replies, passes it to the right person and remembers to follow up. Those connected steps are a workflow — even if they currently happen through email, a notebook or a spreadsheet.

WHY IT MATTERS

Make the work easier to see.

Mapping a workflow helps reveal repeated tasks, missing information, delays and handovers that can be improved. Sometimes the best fix is simply a clearer process. Sometimes a digital tool can take care of a repetitive step.

Example: handling a new enquiry

Here is a simple example of how a business might organise incoming enquiries.

01

Trigger

A customer submits an enquiry form or sends a message.

02

Capture

The customer's details are recorded in an agreed place.

03

Acknowledge

An acknowledgement is sent so the customer knows it arrived.

04

Notify

The right person is notified and can review the request.

05

Follow up

A reminder is created if the enquiry still needs a response.

What can be automated?

  • Copying form details into a spreadsheet or CRM
  • Sending a standard acknowledgement
  • Notifying a team member about a new request
  • Creating a follow-up reminder or task

What still needs a person?

  • Understanding unusual or sensitive requests
  • Making decisions that need judgement
  • Providing a personal response or quote
  • Checking that the process is working as intended
Automation is not the same as a workflow. A workflow is the process itself. Automation means using tools to carry out selected steps. Not every step should be automated, and not every business needs complex software.
